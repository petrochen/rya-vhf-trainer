# RYA VHF SRC Handbook 2019: Key Points

This note condenses the book into product-ready text for building drills, tests, and scenarios.

## Core Message Types

- `Distress`: grave and imminent danger to a vessel, aircraft, vehicle, or person.
- `Urgency`: urgent safety matter that does not yet meet the distress threshold.
- `Safety`: navigational or meteorological safety information.
- `Routine`: normal operational traffic between vessels, marinas, harbours, and coast stations.

## Key Channels

- `Channel 16`: distress, safety, and calling by voice.
- `Channel 70`: DSC only. No voice traffic.
- Routine calls should move off `16` onto a working channel as soon as contact is made.
- The channel chart in the appendix highlights published inter-ship, port operations, and SAR channels.
- The handbook chart marks `Channel 67` for UK small-craft safety / HM Coastguard SAR use.

## Routine Voice Procedure

- Listen before transmitting.
- Think before speaking.
- Use plain English.
- Identify who you are calling and who you are.
- Use `Over` only when a reply is expected.
- Use `Out` when the exchange is complete.
- Do not say `Over and out`.
- Use the phonetic alphabet when spelling names, callsigns, or ambiguous words.

## Standard Procedure Words

- `This is`: identifies your station.
- `Over`: end of transmission, reply expected.
- `Out`: end of transmission, no reply expected.
- `Say again`: repeat what you just said.
- `Read back`: repeat my message back to me.
- `Radio check`: report signal strength and readability.
- `I spell`: next word will be spelt phonetically.
- `Correction`: I am correcting what I just said.
- `Wait`: pause, often qualified by time if possible.

## Distress Procedure

The distress call:

- `Mayday, Mayday, Mayday`
- `This is`
- vessel name x3
- callsign
- MMSI if available

The distress message follows immediately and carries the important information. The book uses the mnemonic `MIPDANIO`:

- `M`: Mayday
- `I`: identity
- `P`: position
- `D`: distress
- `A`: assistance required
- `N`: number of persons on board
- `I`: information useful to rescuers
- `O`: over

Important exam rule:

- A DSC distress alert should be followed immediately by a Mayday voice call and message on `Channel 16`.

## Distress Classification Examples

- Flooding fast, fire, collision, capsize risk, or man overboard with grave and imminent danger: `Distress`.
- Engine failure drifting toward danger but not yet in grave and imminent danger: `Urgency`.
- Medical advice request through the Coastguard: `Urgency`.
- Hazard to navigation, weather warning, floating obstruction: `Safety`.
- Berth request, routine harbour coordination, normal yacht-to-yacht contact: `Routine`.

## Urgency And Safety

Urgency:

- proword `Pan Pan` repeated three times
- usually on `Channel 16`
- followed immediately by the urgency message

Safety:

- proword `Securite` repeated three times
- used for navigational or meteorological safety information
- announced on `16`, message passed on a working channel

## DSC Essentials

- `MMSI` is the nine-digit identity of the radio installation.
- DSC categories map to voice priorities:
  - `Distress` -> `Mayday`
  - `Urgency` -> `Pan Pan`
  - `Safety` -> `Securite`
  - `Routine` -> routine calling
- A DSC controller should ideally be interfaced with GPS so position and time are available automatically.
- Urgency and safety DSC calls still require a follow-up voice message.
- Distress alerts must not be sent accidentally; false alert procedures matter.

## Product Implications For Training Site

Good drills based on the book:

- classify a scenario as distress / urgency / safety / routine
- choose the right channel
- assemble a Mayday / Pan Pan / Securite transmission in the correct order
- choose the correct standard procedure word
- practise phonetic spelling of boat names, callsigns, and MMSI digits
- simulate DSC flow: alert -> voice follow-up -> acknowledgement expectations
- identify incorrect procedures such as voice on Channel 70 or staying on 16 for a long routine call
